    What my PhD is about

    In plain words

    Most things we buy follow a straight line: materials are extracted, made into products, used for a while and thrown away. A circular economy tries to turn that line into a loop, so that products are repaired, reused and recycled and their materials come back as input for something new.

    No single company can close a loop on its own. A used plastic crate only becomes a new product if someone collects it, someone sorts it, someone recycles it and someone else buys the recycled material. I study these chains of companies and organisations in Limburg and help build them, with tools such as roadmaps and games that bring the people involved around the table.

    For students

    A value chain covers every step that adds value to a product, from raw material to end user. In a circular value chain the end-of-life stage feeds back into production, which raises new questions: who owns the returned material, who pays for collection and sorting, and when can secondary materials compete with virgin ones on price?

    Limburg is an interesting case because it combines heavy industry, such as the chemical cluster around Chemelot, with a large construction sector and a strong manufacturing industry. The province aims to halve its use of primary raw materials by 2030 and to have a fully circular economy by 2050. My research supports this with circular economy tools: roadmaps that set out how a value chain can become circular, and serious games in which companies and policymakers explore pathways together.

    For researchers

    The project asks how, and under what conditions, participatory systems analysis, serious gaming and computer modelling can strengthen regional circular economy roadmapping by supporting social learning, Dual Track Governance measures and coordinated action. It covers the three ecosystems of the CEL4LIFE programme (circular chemistry, construction and manufacturing) and focuses on five product and material groups: circular packaging, battery recycling, plastics recycling, circular concrete and bio-based construction.

    Regional roadmaps are developed with the seven-step method of Mahanty et al. (2026), based on stakeholder workshops and text mining of national and regional roadmaps. A serious game is evaluated for its ability to promote social learning, and computer simulations based on causal loop diagrams are run for two or three product groups. The thesis will consist of three or four empirical articles.
